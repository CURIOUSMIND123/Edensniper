    The method, in his words, for Nifty and Sensex

    Source: JJ Simon on Chart Fanatics (US Nasdaq futures, prop-firm accounts). He calls it fair pricing theory. The session's opening price is fair. The burst of orders at the open pushes price away from it, and price tends to come back. Everything is on the 1-minute chart, and every signal waits for the candle to close.

    1. Fair price = the 9:15 opening price. On a scheduled news day (RBI policy at 10:00, Budget) the fair price for after the news is the price just before it. After surprise news, it is where price settles and goes sideways after the spike.
    2. Bias = the opposite of the last session. If today opened above yesterday's 9:15 open, the bias is shorts. If it opened below, longs. He "inverts the previous 6 to 12 hours".
    3. Trade 1, the opening candle: at the close of the 9:15 candle, trade in its colour, but only if that agrees with the bias. If the candle is bigger than your stop, double the stop and target and use half the lots. On Nifty the first minute was bigger than 20 points on every test day, so this trade is normally 40 / 60 points (Sensex 120 / 180). That's about ₹1,400 of risk on one lot. It was the best part of the test, but it's also the biggest single risk of your day.
    4. After that, only trades back toward the fair price. Wait for price to move away (at least 80% of the target). Enter when a candle closes beyond the latest swing: for a buy, above the last swing high (a candle whose high is above the candles on each side). For a sell, below the last swing low. He calls this the stronger entry, and it's the only one he uses on his own money.
    5. Displacement (optional, weaker): a candle whose body is bigger than the previous one, closing beyond the previous candle's wick, and the previous candle was the opposite colour. It lost money in our test, so it's off by default.
    6. Fixed stop and target, 1 : 1.5. His 25 / 38 and 50 / 76 points on Nasdaq scale to about 10 / 15 and 20 / 30 Nifty points (Nasdaq moves about 2.4 times as many points in the first 90 minutes). The 10-point version lost money after charges, so use Nifty 20 / 30 and Sensex 60 / 90.
    7. Only the first 90 minutes: new trades 9:15 to 10:45. A trade still open keeps its stop and target, and is closed by 3:15.
    8. Stop for the day after losses in a row. His rule is 3. Use 2 with ₹30k: it made no difference in the test and caps a bad day.
    9. One trade at a time. He spreads trades over 45 prop accounts; you have one account, so you take the same signal once.

    Morning routine

    1. Before 9:15. Note yesterday's 9:15 open. Check for news: an RBI policy day, the Budget, or a big overnight move in the US.
    2. 9:15. The Fair Price indicator draws the fair price line, and its table shows the bias.
    3. 9:16. The 9:15 candle has closed. If its colour matches the bias, that's trade 1. Buy the ATM call (green) or put (red).
    4. 9:16 to 10:45. Let price run away from the fair price. When a BUY or SELL label prints, screenshot the chart, check the plan here, and enter.
    5. Exit only at the stop or target (or 3:15). Mark the result in Today. After 2 losses in a row, close the app for the day.

    Trade the option, not futures: one Nifty futures lot needs well over ₹1 lakh of margin. One ATM option lot costs about ₹6,000 to ₹10,000. With charges, a stopped break-of-structure trade loses about ₹800 to ₹1,200, and a stopped opening trade about ₹1,400. A bad day (two losses, then stop) costs about ₹2,200, which is 7% of ₹30k.

    What the test showed

    These are his rules run on the last 19 trading days of real 1-minute Nifty and Sensex data (4 Sep to 1 Oct 2026). Every trade is one lot, stopping after 2 losses in a row. Option profit is estimated at delta 0.5 after ₹130 per lot of charges and slippage. "Random" is the share of 500 runs where coin-flip directions, with the same entries, stops and targets, did as well or better. Lower means the direction calls mattered.

    • The opening trade made most of the profit, but that's only 12 trades (7 won). A few bad mornings in a row would change the picture.

    • His own profits come from prop firms: he risks about $100 per evaluation, not his own capital, and says this fixed-target version is tuned for prop-firm rules, not for a live account.
    • SEBI found that about 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lose money. Paper-trade this for two to four weeks first. Then trade one lot, and only with money you can afford to lose.
